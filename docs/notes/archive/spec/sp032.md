# Spec [[cat001]] [[cat002]] [[board]]

## extends [[ft016]]

## problem
[[ft016]] is driven with the keyboard only, and two of its three panes cannot
be read when their content outgrows them. Both gaps are in the SHELL — the
event loop, the focus model and the scroll — not in what the monitor reads or
in what [[ft012]] and [[ft014]] publish. No new data source appears here.

**The mouse does nothing.** Every keystroke path exists (`tab`, `jk`, arrows,
`/`, `d`), and none of them is reachable by pointer: a click lands in the
terminal's own selection, a wheel event scrolls the terminal's scrollback
rather than the pane under the pointer. An operator watching a roster of a
dozen agents and a log that grows every two seconds navigates entirely by
repeated `j`, with no way to jump to the row they are already looking at.

**A long message cannot be read at all.** [[sp031]]'s detail pane is capped at
roughly a third of the terminal height or eight rows, whichever is smaller,
and a body past that cap is truncated with a visible indicator. The indicator
is honest and the cap protects the two panes above it, but between them they
mean a result envelope of two hundred lines of JSON is permanently unreadable
inside the tool that exists to show it. The detail pane also takes no focus,
so no key and no wheel reaches its body even when the operator wants nothing
else on screen. The message log is scrollable in principle — its cursor
derives its scroll — but it has no page, home or end motion, so reaching an
old message in a long history is one `k` at a time.

### the shell moves to bubbletea, the renderers do not
The chosen shape is a Charm shell around the EXISTING renderers.
`cmd/agent-monitor` builds a `tea.Program` with the alternate screen and cell
motion enabled; each pane becomes a `bubbles` viewport whose content is the
plain text `internal/render`'s `Render`, `RenderLog` and `RenderDetail`
already produce, unchanged. The column layout, the drop-from-the-right
priority, the subject derivation, the C0/C1/DEL neutraliser and the cell-aware
truncation all stay exactly where they are and keep the tests that pin them.
This is deliberate and is the binding constraint on the task breakdown: those
rules are load-bearing per [[ft016]]'s `## data_model` — the pane displays
content written by OTHER agents — and a rewrite onto `bubbles/list` item
delegates and `lipgloss` styling would re-open every one of them for no
interaction gain.

`--once` is the second binding constraint. It never constructs a
`tea.Program`: it keeps today's code path and its contract that the byte
stream a pipe receives contains nothing a terminal would interpret as a
control sequence. [[sp030]] T9 asserts this and the assertion stays as-is.

What the shell DOES take over is what it is good at: raw mode and its restore
(including on panic, retiring `tui.Restorer`'s `sync.Once` and the hand-rolled
`readKeys` goroutine), `SIGWINCH`, escape decoding (retiring `tui.Decoder`),
mouse reports, and per-pane scroll. The two samplers stay in
`internal/source` with [[adr0014]]'s guards intact and deliver into the loop
as messages rather than by calling a draw callback on their own goroutine —
which also removes the reason `tui.Restorer.Guard` existed. `tui.Model`'s
cursor, filter, `--project` composition and focus logic are PORTED onto
`tea.KeyMsg`, not rewritten.

### the interaction contract this adds
Mouse: a left click on a data row focuses that pane and moves its cursor to
the clicked row (the detail pane follows for a message row); a click on a
pane header focuses that pane only; the wheel scrolls the pane UNDER THE
POINTER by three lines without moving focus. Keyboard: `tab` cycles three
stops, roster then messages then detail, and the detail pane's body scrolls
with `jk`, the arrows, `PgUp`/`PgDn` and the wheel; `enter` or `o` zooms the
detail to the full screen for a long payload and `esc` returns to the
three-pane layout; `PgUp`, `PgDn`, `Home`, `End` and `G` work on all three
panes. `d` still toggles the detail pane off, and `q`/`Q`/`Ctrl-C`, `r`/`R`,
`/` (still swallowing every rune while a draft is open), `--once` and
`--project` are unchanged.

The log tail-follows CONDITIONALLY: when the cursor sits on the newest
message it follows new arrivals, and once the operator has scrolled back at
all the position is pinned and the pane header carries a `+N new` count until
`End`/`G` returns to live. Unconditional follow would yank a reader off the
message they are reading every two seconds; never following would make the
live view stop being live.

Enabling mouse reporting COSTS the terminal's own click-drag selection, and
that cost is accepted rather than worked around: `Shift`+drag still selects in
both WezTerm and tmux, and [[ft016]] will say so where it documents the keys.

### what this decides that has no record yet
Adopting `bubbletea`, `bubbles` and `lipgloss` makes this the first vendored
TUI framework in the repo, against a binary that today requires `x/term`
alone. [[adr0030]] admits Go for this one interactive TUI on its own merits
and says nothing about frameworks, so nothing here overturns it; [[adr0011]]
is the standing bespoke-over-vendor precedent but it ruled on clipboard
managers rather than on rendering libraries, so this does not overturn that
either. It is nonetheless a dependency decision with consequences a future
proposal will want to cite — a new `adr####` records it at `spec-retro`, and
this spec does not pretend an existing ADR already covers it. [[adr0031]] is
untouched: the consumer still formats its own display and the transport still
interprets nothing, and the SUBJECT column stays underived while only the
detail pane formats.

### out of scope
Read-only stands: no verb reaches a worker, because `resume` / `stop` /
`accept` destroy worktrees, branches and addresses and stay with whoever
allocated them. No column resize and no sort. `dotfiles-1xfo` — the
interactive filter matching `From` only, so filtering to a worker's name hides
every message addressed to it — is real and already filed, and stays filed
rather than riding along here.

## solution

A shell-layer change to [[ft016]] alone. No `ft###` is minted — this is a
feature refresh declared by `## extends`, the same shape [[sp031]] used, so
[[ft016]]'s card is rewritten at `spec-retro` to match what ships rather than a
second Feature appearing beside it. No `us###` or `im###` is invented to
satisfy a story-backed path that does not apply here. Nothing reads a new
source: [[ft012]]'s census rows and [[ft014]]'s `messages` verb arrive exactly
as they do today, on the same three clocks, and no verb, probe or bus layout
moves. Categories [[cat001]] and [[cat002]] are unchanged.

**`bubbletea` becomes the shell; `internal/render` keeps owning every byte.**
That seam is the whole solution and every other choice below follows from it.
`cmd/agent-monitor` builds a `tea.Program` with the alternate screen and cell
motion enabled, and every pane's CONTENT stays the plain text `render.Render`,
`render.RenderLog` and `render.RenderDetail` already produce — unchanged, with
their tests unchanged.

`bubbles/viewport` is used for the DETAIL pane only, and `spec-refinement`
narrowed it there deliberately after reading the scroll model. A viewport
carries its own `YOffset`, and the roster and message panes already derive
their scroll from a cursor on every `SetLen` — that is, every two-second
sample. Two scroll authorities over the same pane means the derive yanks the
viewport's offset back on the next tick, and the failure would be
intermittent rather than obvious. Those two panes therefore keep the cursor
model that is already tested, extended by Task 1 so the scroll offset becomes
first-class state that a wheel can move without dragging the cursor with it.
The detail pane has no cursor and no selection, so a viewport is exactly what
it wants and nothing arbitrates against it. `lipgloss` is used for
the frame's focus marking only and never to lay out a pane's content. The
column set and its drop-from-the-right priority, the subject derivation, the
C0/C1/DEL neutraliser and the display-cell truncation that never cuts
mid-escape all stay where they are. Those rules are load-bearing rather than
cosmetic per [[ft016]]'s `## data_model` — this UI displays content written by
OTHER agents — and re-expressing them as `bubbles/list` item delegates would
re-open every one of them in exchange for no interaction the viewport approach
does not already give. Keeping render's output as plain text is also what keeps
its "no line exceeds the width" tests measuring real text rather than escape
bytes.

**The framework is admitted for the four things it is actually better at**:
mouse reporting and its decoding, per-pane scroll, `SIGWINCH`, and raw-mode
restore including on panic. Those retire `tui.Decoder`, `tui.Restorer` and the
hand-rolled `readKeys` goroutine. [[adr0030]] admits Go for this one
interactive TUI on its own merits and says nothing about frameworks, so nothing
here reopens it — and [[adr0003]]'s gate is not touched or stretched either.
[[adr0011]] is the repo's standing bespoke-over-vendor precedent, but its
foreclosure is scoped to clipboard backends on a secrets-adjacent surface and
does not bind a rendering library; this spec deliberately ships the dependency
WITHOUT minting an ADR for it, as an explicit decision rather than an
oversight. A later proposal that wants the framework choice as precedent should
know it was taken here and recorded here, not in the decision log.

**The samplers keep their goroutines and [[adr0014]]'s guards keep their
tests.** `source.RunLoop` and `source.RunMessagesLoop` are not rewritten as
`tea.Cmd` tickers: they keep fail-fast on unrecoverable setup, the per-iteration
sleep floor and the bounded retry outside the loop, exactly as written and
asserted. Only the callback changes — what was a `draw()` call becomes a
`p.Send`. `tui.Restorer`'s `sync.Once` and its `Guard` retire as a CONSEQUENCE
of that, not because panic safety became less important: with rendering no
longer happening on a sampler's goroutine, the specific hazard `Guard` existed
for (one goroutine's panic leaving the terminal in raw mode because Go never
runs another goroutine's defers) stops existing, and `bubbletea` covers the
remaining case. `tui.Model`'s cursor, filter and `--project` composition are
PORTED onto `tea.KeyMsg`, not redesigned.

**`--once` never constructs a `tea.Program`.** It keeps today's code path and
its contract that the byte stream a pipe receives contains nothing a terminal
would interpret as a control sequence — [[sp030]] T9 asserts this and the
assertion stands unmodified. A frame that composes in a pipe and in a tmux
status line is a separate output mode, not the interactive renderer with colour
disabled, and treating it as the latter is how a `lipgloss`-everywhere design
would have leaked an escape byte into it.

**The detail pane becomes a peer of the other two.** `tab` cycles three stops,
and a focused detail body scrolls with `jk`, the arrows, `PgUp`/`PgDn` and the
wheel; `enter`/`o` zooms it to the full screen for a long payload and `esc`
returns. The `min(height/3, 8)` cap survives in the three-pane layout rather
than being relaxed now that the pane scrolls: `dotfiles-m0km` already cost one
round of an unbudgeted frame scrolling the roster off the top invisibly, and a
scrollable region is not a reason to let a third pane take the screen. `d` still
hides it. `PgUp`, `PgDn`, `Home`, `End` and `G` work on all three panes, which
is what makes a long message history reachable at all — today it is one `k` per
row.

**The log tail-follows conditionally, and that is a rule rather than a
default.** When the cursor sits on the newest message it follows new arrivals;
once the operator has scrolled back at all the position is pinned and the pane
header carries a `+N new` count until `End`/`G` returns to live. Unconditional
follow would yank a reader off the message they are reading every two seconds,
and never following would stop the live view being live. Stated here so neither
half is later "fixed" into the other.

**Enabling mouse reporting costs the terminal's own click-drag selection**, and
that cost is taken rather than engineered around: `Shift`+drag still selects in
both WezTerm and tmux, and [[ft016]] will document it beside the keys. A
modifier-gated or toggleable mouse mode was considered and rejected as a second
input state to explain and to test for a problem the terminal already solves.

Unchanged and explicitly out of scope: [[adr0031]]'s boundary (the consumer
formats its own display, the transport interprets nothing — the SUBJECT column
stays underived and only the detail pane formats), [[adr0017]]'s observational
`unknown`, [[adr0024]] and [[adr0028]]'s split surfacing in the `PROJECT`
column, [[adr0030]]'s no-nushell-wrapper carve-out, and the read-only stance —
no verb reaches a worker. `dotfiles-1xfo` stays filed rather than folded in. No
column resize and no sort.

`internal/source/` appearing with no task against it is the point, not an

spec changes what a tick DOES with its result, never how a tick is clocked or
guarded.

### conventions

- **`render/` emits text, never escapes.** Focus marking, reverse video and
  any `lipgloss` styling are applied in `cmd/` after rendering, as
  `markPane` already does. This is what keeps render's "no line exceeds the
  width" tests measuring real text, and it is why `lipgloss` never touches a
  pane's content.
- **One arithmetic for pane sizes.** `paneBudgets` is the single source of
  truth for how many rows each region gets. T3's layout DERIVES from it; it
  does not re-implement it. Two derivations that agree today drift tomorrow.
- **`height <= 0` means "do not clamp".** Already the contract for
  `paneBudgets` and `buildFrame`; T4 extends the same convention to
  `RenderDetail` rather than inventing a second spelling.
- **Every new key goes through `Model`, not through `Update`.** `Update` maps
  a `tea.KeyMsg` onto a `Model` method and does nothing else, so the key
  surface stays unit-testable without a `tea.Program`.
- **Mouse coordinates are converted to (pane, row) in ONE place** — the
  layout's hit test — and every mouse handler consumes that result. No
  handler does its own arithmetic on `MouseMsg.Y`.

### anti-patterns

- **Do not route roster or message scrolling through `bubbles/viewport`.**
  Two scroll authorities over one pane, with `SetLen` re-deriving every two
  seconds; see `## solution`. The detail pane is the only viewport.
- **Do not "fix" conditional tail-follow into unconditional follow** because
  a test looked easier, and do not drop the `+N new` counter as cosmetic: it
  is the only signal that the frozen pane is frozen on purpose.
- **Do not let `lipgloss` render a pane's columns.** The drop-from-the-right
  column priority, the cell-aware truncation and the C0/C1/DEL neutraliser
  are load-bearing ([[ft016]] `## data_model`) because this UI displays bytes
  other agents wrote.
- **Do not parse message content anywhere outside `RenderDetail`.**
  [[adr0031]]: the SUBJECT column stays raw-first-line, underived. A JSON
  payload rendering as an ugly `{` in the log is correct and is asserted so.
- **Do not make `--once` a styled render with colour disabled.** It
  constructs no `tea.Program` at all. A `lipgloss` colour-profile switch is
  not the same guarantee as never emitting an escape byte.
- **Do not convert the samplers to `tea.Cmd` tickers.** [[adr0014]]'s guards
  and their tests live in `source.RunLoop` / `RunMessagesLoop`; only the
  callback changes.
- **Do not leave a retired symbol unused.** `Decoder`, `Restorer`,
  `readKeys`, `enterInteractiveMode`, `writeFrame`, `frameBytes` are deleted
  in T2, not orphaned — a dead raw-mode path is the one most likely to be
  resurrected by a later "restore this" commit.

### known limitations

- Mouse reporting takes the terminal's own click-drag selection for the
  duration of the session. `Shift`+drag still selects in WezTerm and tmux.
  Accepted in `## solution`; no in-app copy affordance is added.
- Nothing in this repo has read a mouse report before, and the confidence
  gate was deliberately skipped (no `poc###`). If wheel or click events do
  not survive the WezTerm → WSL → tmux → alt-screen path, T3 is where that
  surfaces; its criteria are written so the failure is loud rather than
  silently inert.
- `dotfiles-1xfo` (the interactive filter matches `From` only) is untouched
  and stays filed separately.
- The census `PROJECT` column keeps reporting the display host rather than
  the bus slug ([[adr0024]], [[adr0028]]); no task narrows that.
- No column resize, no sort, and the monitor stays read-only — no verb
  reaches a worker.

### Task 1: Scroll becomes first-class state, the cursor only ensures visibility

#### type
refactor

#### effort
5h

#### depends
none

#### files_touched
- `agent-monitor/internal/tui/keys.go`
- `agent-monitor/internal/tui/keys_test.go`
- `agent-monitor/cmd/agent-monitor/main.go` (call sites of `SetRosterLen` /
  `SetMessagesLen` / `SetRosterViewport` / `SetMessagesViewport`)

#### success_criteria
1. `Model` exposes `ScrollRoster(delta int)` and `ScrollMessages(delta int)`
   that move that pane's scroll offset WITHOUT moving its cursor, clamped to
   `[0, max(0, len-viewport)]`.
2. `SetRosterLen` / `SetMessagesLen` CLAMP the current scroll into range
   instead of re-deriving it from the cursor. A scroll set by `ScrollX`
   survives any number of subsequent `SetLen` calls at unchanged length —
   this is the premise both the wheel (T3) and the frozen tail (T6) rest on.
3. Cursor motion adjusts scroll by the MINIMUM needed to bring the cursor
   back inside `[scroll, scroll+viewport-1]`; a cursor already on screen
   leaves scroll byte-identical.
4. `SetRosterViewport` / `SetMessagesViewport` still clamp scroll, so a
   shrunk terminal cannot leave scroll past the last full page.
5. `go test ./...` passes in `agent-monitor/`. Pre-existing `keys_test.go`
   cases are edited ONLY where they assert derive-from-cursor semantics that
   criterion 2 deliberately replaces; each such edit is justified in the
   commit body.

#### edge_cases
- Empty list (`len == 0`): scroll pinned at 0, no negative clamp.
- `viewport == 0` (the `--once` path never reports one): scroll tracks the
  cursor 1:1, exactly as today — this is the compatibility contract for
  every caller that sets `*Len` and never `*Viewport`.
- `viewport >= len`: scroll is always 0 regardless of wheel input.
- A committed filter shrinks the list under a scrolled view: scroll and
  cursor both clamp in the same pass, so `scrolledMessageSample`'s
  `msgs[scroll:]` cannot panic.
- Cursor deliberately off-screen (post-wheel) and then the list shrinks.

#### test_plan
- `TestScroll_WheelScrollSurvivesASampleTick` — `ScrollMessages(3)`, then
  five `SetMessagesLen(n)` at unchanged `n`; scroll is still 3. Fails today.
- `TestScroll_CursorOffScreenIsAllowed` — the state the old model forbade.
- `TestCursor_MoveOnlyScrollsWhenItMustEnsureVisibility` — table over cursor
  positions inside and outside the window; asserts scroll UNCHANGED for the
  inside cases, not merely valid.
- `TestScroll_ClampsWhenFilterShrinksTheList` — then a real
  `scrolledMessageSample` slice, so the clamp is proven at the use site.
- `TestScroll_ViewportZeroKeepsLegacyOneToOneTracking`.
- `TestScroll_NeverExceedsMaxForViewport` — randomised deltas.

### Task 3: pane geometry and the mouse

#### type
feature

#### effort
6h

#### depends
Task 2

#### files_touched
- `agent-monitor/cmd/agent-monitor/main.go` (layout + hit test)
- `agent-monitor/cmd/agent-monitor/main_test.go`
- `agent-monitor/internal/tui/keys.go` (mouse entry points on `Model`)
- `agent-monitor/internal/tui/keys_test.go`

#### success_criteria
1. `renderFrame` returns, beside its lines, a layout value naming each
   VISIBLE pane's first screen row, its header height and its data-row
   count — derived from `paneBudgets`, never re-implementing that
   arithmetic.
2. A left-button press inside a pane's data rows focuses that pane and sets
   its cursor to `scroll + (y - firstDataRow)`; a press on a pane header or
   column-header row focuses without moving the cursor; a press on a
   separator or past the last data row changes nothing.
3. Wheel up/down scrolls the pane UNDER THE POINTER by 3 lines through Task
   1's `ScrollX`, leaving focus and every cursor untouched — including over
   an unfocused pane.
4. Clicking a message row updates the detail pane in the SAME frame.
5. No mouse event produces a cursor outside `[0, len-1]` or a scroll outside
   its clamp, for any `(x, y)` at any terminal size.

#### edge_cases
- Click below the last row of a short pane; click on the blank separator.
- Click on a pane showing `(no agents)` / `(no messages)`: focus only, never
  a selection of the placeholder line.
- Click while `Editing` a filter: focus and selection move, the draft is NOT
  cancelled.
- Wheel over the detail pane while it is hidden, and wheel over a pane with
  zero rows.
- `x` beyond the rendered width, and `y == 0`.
- `MouseActionMotion` events (cell motion reports them during a drag) are
  ignored, never treated as presses.

  the layout's rows equal what `paneBudgets` allocated.
- `TestMouse_ClickOnDataRowSelectsThatRow` — table over pane × row × scroll
