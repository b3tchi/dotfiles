# ADR [[cat001]] [[product]]

## title
a consumer may format what it displays; the log's subject column stays underived

## context
[[ft014]]'s opacity rule says the bus "carries any consumer's vocabulary and
gates on none of it, including the shape of `content`". [[sp030]] T9 turned
that into a concrete anti-pattern for [[ft016]]: the message log's SUBJECT
column must not parse `content` to prettify it, so a JSON body renders as its
own ugly first line and a test pins that so nobody "fixes" it.

[[sp031]] then added a detail pane that pretty-prints a JSON body. That looks
like a direct contradiction of the rule, and it was argued as one twice during
execution — once when T4's renderer was audited, and again when
`dotfiles-56lh` was diagnosed and the obvious repair was to make the subject
column sniff content shape instead of fixing the transport.

The distinction that resolves it was stated in [[sp031]]'s own `## problem`
but was never recorded where a future consumer would find it: [[ft014]]'s rule
binds the TRANSPORT, and `agent-monitor` is a CONSUMER. A transport that
inspected `content` would gate on a vocabulary it does not own. A consumer
formatting bytes already addressed to it gates nothing — the envelope reached
its destination either way.

Without this written down, the rule reads as "nobody may ever format content",
which is both wrong and actively harmful: it argues for fixing a lying `kind`
field in the renderer rather than in the writer, which is exactly the repair
`dotfiles-56lh` had to refuse.

## decision
A consumer MAY format content it has been given, for display only. The
transport MUST NOT inspect, parse or gate on `content`.

Within `agent-monitor` specifically, the boundary sits between two renderers
and does not move:

- the log's SUBJECT column stays raw-first-line and underived — one line per
  envelope, derived only by the rule in [[ft016]]'s `## data_model`, with
  tests pinning the JSON-blob case;
- the DETAIL pane formats the selected envelope's body: pretty-printed when it
  parses as JSON, raw when it does not, parse failure falling back to raw
  rather than erroring.

Type is read from the envelope's `kind` field, never inferred from the shape
of `content`. A consumer that cannot tell a state envelope from prose must fix
the writer, not sniff the payload. No `subject` field is ever added to an
envelope to make display easier.

## consequences
A renderer may be as readable as it likes without touching [[ft014]]'s
contract, and `dotfiles-56lh`'s real defect — results reaching the bus stamped
with the wrong `kind` — had to be fixed in the transport where it belonged
rather than papered over in the renderer. [[dotfiles-oj4c]] later made that
structural: `kind` names the shape of `content` by rule and the writer refuses
a mismatch, so the discrimination this ADR depends on cannot silently rot.

The cost is that "may a consumer parse this?" now has two answers depending on
which side of the boundary the code sits, and the boundary is a judgement
about role rather than a mechanical test. The compensating control is that the
underived column keeps its tests: any future change that makes the SUBJECT
column parse content fails them, and that failure is the signal that someone
has moved the boundary without deciding to.

Supersedes nothing. [[adr0028]]'s addressing and transport clauses are
untouched; this ADR narrows only what "opacity" obliges of a reader.
