# ADR [[cat002]] [[product]]

## title
screen corners and cutouts are machine data from gmobile; the bars cover them, tiles stay rectangular

## context
The Nothing Phone's panel has rounded corners and a front-camera hole
near its top-left. With kwi3 tiling the whole screen, the bar's ends ran
under the corner curves, the camera sat on the first window's titlebar,
and the bottom tiles' corners were cut by the curve. Linux exposes none
of this: DRM reports a rectangle, and KWin offers no cutout data to a
layer-shell client. phosh's gmobile project keeps exactly this per
phone model (`data/devices/display-panels/<compatible>.json`: corner
radius, cutout paths, in physical px), but gmobile is not installed on
the phone and pulling it in only for a data file is heavy. Measuring by
eye with a calibration overlay put the corner between r=70 and r=80
logical, where gmobile says 107 physical (~67 logical).

## decision
A screen's panel geometry is machine data in the machine file
([[adr0037]]): `corner` (radius, logical px) and `panel` (gmobile's
`x-res` and its cutouts as rects in PHYSICAL px, copied from gmobile
with its source named), scaled by x-res over the screen's logical width.
The bars absorb it instead of the tiler: a cutout in the top half moves
the top bar's row down until it ends level with the hole's bottom, black
above it, so the hole sits inside the bar; the bottom bar reaches up at
least the corner radius, its row right under the tiles. Both bars keep
the desktop's spacing to the tiles (the row hugs them, the filler goes
to the screen edge), and a row's ends stay clear of the corner curve at
its distance from the edge, in whole cells on kwi3's grid.

## consequences
kwi3's tiler stays rectangular: it only sees a larger exclusive zone, so
no notch shapes in Logic. Panel numbers come from a maintained source and
stay checkable against it; a measured value (Jan's 75 over gmobile's 67)
can override. The cost is screen height: about 30 px of black across the
top and ~46 px below the bottom row on the phone, full width, because a
rectangular work area cannot step around a hole. The copy of gmobile's
data does not follow upstream fixes, and only rect cutouts are read, not
gmobile's general SVG paths.
