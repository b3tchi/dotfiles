# ADR [[cat001]] [[product]]

## title
display may write when the verb destroys nothing

## context
[[ft016]] shipped with a flat stance in its card: "Read-only: no verb reaches a
worker", justified by what `resume` / `stop` / `accept` do — they destroy
worktrees, branches and addresses, and belong to whoever allocated them.

That stance was correct while every party on the bus was a process someone
spawned: a display cannot usefully answer on a worker's behalf. It stopped
being correct the moment a PERSON became a first-class party. `pi-worker
register` mints an address with `kind: person`, and a human now receives mail
that only they can answer — while the one tool that renders the bus could show
the conversation and do nothing about it.

[[sp033]] gave [[ft016]] a reply composer, which makes it a writer. The word
"read-only" would then be false, and the temptation was to delete it.

## decision
A display MAY write to the bus, provided the verb it calls destroys nothing and
allocates nothing. [[ft016]]'s stance is narrowed, not dropped: **no DESTRUCTIVE
verb reaches a worker from a display.**

Concretely, `send` is admitted; `resume`, `stop`, `accept`, `rm` and `reclaim`
remain out of reach from any rendering consumer. The line is ownership, not
mutation: those five destroy or reassign a resource and stay with whoever
allocated it, while `send` appends an envelope and confers nothing.

Two constraints ride with the admission, both already binding and both
re-affirmed here because a writer is where they would first be broken:

- the written body travels opaque ([[adr0031]]) as `kind: message`
  ([[adr0032]]) — a consumer formats what it DISPLAYS and interprets nothing it
  SENDS;
- the recipient is the minted ADDRESS carried in the envelope, never a
  re-resolved label ([[adr0034]]), and it is bound when the composer opens
  rather than when it commits, so a list that reorders under the cursor cannot
  redirect a reply.

## consequences
[[ft016]]'s card says "no DESTRUCTIVE verb reaches a worker" and names the
reason, so the next reader inherits the boundary rather than the slogan.

The boundary is now a judgement rather than a category: a future verb has to be
classified before a display may call it. `send` was easy; something like a
hypothetical `nudge` or `retry` would need arguing. That cost is accepted — the
alternative was a rule that was already false in practice.

A display that writes can misattribute. `--as` is an unauthenticated string
(`dotfiles-btkt.4`), so an envelope from [[ft016]] claims an identity it cannot
prove, exactly as the CLI does. This ADR does not change that; it records that
a writer makes the gap matter more, because a human now reads those envelopes
as coming from another human.
